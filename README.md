# LONGHAUL

Hard science-fiction ship-captain game for the browser. You pick a ship (anything from a Belt prospecting skiff to a surplus Martian corvette) and command it and its crew in a solar system divided between the United Nations, the Martian Congressional Republic and the Outer Planets Alliance. Everything runs on one simulated minute at a time: orbits, burns and flips, crew needs and watches, machinery wear, and torpedoes that can take days to arrive.

No build step, no dependencies: vanilla ES modules, IndexedDB saves, WebGL2 for the 3D pictures (the drone's view, the telescope, the crew on the room cameras; without it they are drawn flat). Nothing is loaded from the network, and the game plays offline: ships, stations, worlds and rooms are made in code, and the crew's bodies come from the game's own folder (`assets/crew/`).

## Play

```sh
./play.sh          # serves the folder on :8090 and opens the browser
```

Keys: `Space` pause · `1/2/3` time warp · `M` the system · `T` local space · `R` scanner · `W` weapons · `X` exterior cameras · `Z` the telescope on what is picked · `C` battle stations · `U` all hands into suits · `E` engineering · `D` duty roster · `L` missions · `J` jobs · `H` comms · `B` station · `V` service & relations · `I` ship's library · `O` the log · `F` flag what is picked · `Esc` the top window · `Ctrl+S` quicksave. Hand-flying a docking: `W/S` close/open, `A/D` left/right, `R/F` up/down, arrows yaw/pitch, `Q/E` roll, `X` kill drift.

**The screen is the ship's flight console.** The main view is the ship's displays: the system (the default), local space, the scanner, weapons, and the exterior cameras (they come up by themselves for a docking, and for our torpedoes' arrival). Under their tabs, the flight band says what the ship is doing (docked, burning, holding), what wants an answer (traffic control, securing for a burn) and the latest messages; nothing is laid over the displays. Click anything on the system display for a card beside it with what it is and what you can do; the CONTEXT panel on the right has the whole picture, the ROUTE panel the planner. Every display, and every list that names a ship, marks the same three things: a ship running dark (no transponder) is drawn hollow and called DARK, what your work points at (the derelict, the convoy, the port to deliver to or report at, a distress call you answered, and the ship you hunt once you know which contact it is: a bounty runs dark, and only its transponder or the drive signature some briefs carry names it) sits in a magenta diamond with the job's name, and `F` puts a lettered yellow flag on whatever you picked, so one unidentified contact can be told from the next. The CONTEXT panel lists your flags; the nav computer calls a flagged contact out when it comes back on the board, and a flag goes when the plot loses the ship. On the left, the SHIP panel (drive, attitude, hull, systems, stores) and CREW & ROOMS, a camera in every compartment: that is where you see the crew (rigged human figures in jumpsuits or vac suits, lit by the room: braced or pinned under hard burns, thrown about when unstrapped, bloodied when hurt) and pick them, or a machine. A contact picked anywhere gets its full sheet in the CONTEXT panel: range, range rate, closest approach, aspect, its drive's G-load, thrust, plume and signature, with the same range and speed the ship panel, the route and the telescope give. Panels collapse, docks resize (and hide on a double click), panels and the exterior cameras float as windows, and every window (a station, comms, engineering, the roster, missions, the library) can be moved and resized and is remembered. Along the bottom, the ship's AI says what needs you, with the button that does it; the radio runs beside it, and the log opens as a drawer. Annunciators on the top rail: a hostile contact or a torpedo inbound, master caution and warning, battle stations, suits.

**Stations are places, and their space is managed.** Ports stand on Ceres, Pallas, Vesta and Phobos at their true radius and turn with them; Hygiea, Tycho, Luna's yards and Ganymede's station orbit their bodies. Every ship and station has a physical hull, built square and practical, with docking collars where its airlocks are. Near a hub the radio is busy: traffic control clears real traffic and warns, cites or shoots at whoever comes in without clearance. Hail it (`H`) to approach, dock and depart. Docking is flown: a final approach down the corridor, then proximity operations second by second on the RCS, by the docking computer or by hand. You can dock with ships that will hold still, trade over the collar, and board ships by collar or EVA. Every hull out there has its crew aboard; what you know of them comes from registries, manifests, hails, scans and boarding, and is logged.

**Gravity, the ground, and port space.** The rocks pull: hovering at Ceres' gate costs thrust, a ship that stops thrusting falls, and the nav computer calls the ground out and climbs round a rock that is in the way. Every port has a holding pattern of 300 slots on a free orbit (over a surface port, the synchronous orbit), where traffic lays over and you can hold for free; inside port space you sign for work and turn in reports without docking. Traffic takes a berth only for the work that needs one. Berths are leased. The nav computer keeps to the ports' speed limits.

**Cameras outside, and the telescope.** Nothing aboard sees the ship from outside. To look at her in her surroundings, deploy a camera drone (the EXTERIOR display's bar; a few in the rack, more bought in port); without one the display is blank. A drone goes out only with the drive cold, and keeps up with the RCS, not the drive: a burn leaves it behind and it is lost, so a route waits for it to come home as it waits for the crew, and EXECUTE NOW goes without it. The drone's picture is the real thing in sunlight: hulls and stations as lit 3D solids built module by module from the same models the sim flies, the rock under a port cratered and shadowed, plumes burning, ports floodlit at night, and every hit a ship has taken where it landed (holes torn through the plating, scorching, peppering from PDC fire, venting air; a wreck dark, tumbling, broken-backed in its cloud of fragments). The telescope (`Z`, or LOOK on a contact or a world) shows one thing from where you are, as the optics would: a hull resolved close in (the solid, lit at its phase), a point of sunlight further out, only its drive plume further still, black sky when there is nothing to see, its night side dark. Its plume filters bring a hull out of its own drive's glare, and let you see, degraded, through your own exhaust. Planets, moons and the Sun (through the solar filter) are drawn as they are, at their phase and turning, all made in code. It holds a ship only to a range (about a million km at TL2). When your torpedoes close on a target it can hold, it comes up by itself, whatever happens to them: nuclear and reactor fireballs, hits, PDC tracers and intercepts are shown as they would look, and a target blown apart is held on.

**Fire and salvage.** Hits can start fires; the crew fight them, or you vent the compartment. The SUITS switch puts everyone in a suit first. A wreck can be salvaged run after run for scrap, spares and stores, and any secured hull's computer cracked for its credits.

**Light is the speed limit on what you know.** Your sensors show what is within about a light-minute, live. The rest of the system is on the solar map as a plot of reports, each with an age: traffic-control digests from every station (berths and filed flight plans, reaching you after the light time from that station), the last fix of every contact that dropped off your board (you can plot a route to where it has coasted, and your sensors take over when they find it again), and news of hot zones and losses. Thousands of ships trade, patrol, blockade and fight out there. Within reach of your sensors they come alive and act for themselves: they fight what they think they can beat and run from what they can't, as crews with bodies would (a sprint at high G, then what juice covers, cold and dark once out of your reach); far away, what happens to them is settled by outcomes, with real wrecks. The `PLOT` button on the system display shows or hides the plot.

**The powers are not at peace.** Earth and Mars live between a cold war and a hot one; the Belt has its own quarrels with both. Each power is a coalition of sub-factions (admirals, spies, doves, companies, militants) that fight each other in the dark, and the powers fight each other through proxies: mercenaries, pirate clans, Belter cells. Pick a **military license** on the new-game screen (or apply for one in port), fly one of its navy's hulls, and you are its contractor: you carry its flag, its enemies are yours, its command ships call you to its fights, and you rise in rank with the work. The `SERVICE` window (`V`) shows your license, rank, how every faction sees you, the hot zones you have heard of (by faction, where guns are wanted first, one click to the map or a route), and the state of the powers.

The **ship's library** (`I`) is the ship computer's reference: every ship class, yard and tech level, the machinery and fittings aboard, weapons, navigation and doctrine, factions and stations, read from the same tables the simulation runs on. It doesn't pause the game; ship classes on the contact and fire-control panels, and machines on the engineering board, link to their pages.

## Test

```sh
npm test                 # all suites
node tests/run.mjs combat   # one suite (substring filter on "file › test name")
```

The tests are integration tests of the simulation (no DOM). They print the numbers that matter for balance (hit rates, flight times, fuel), so read their output, not just the ticks.
