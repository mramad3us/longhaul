# Architecture

## Layers

| Folder | What lives there | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| `js/core/` | clock (integer game-minutes), seeded RNG, event bus, constants | no game knowledge |
| `js/sim/` | the whole simulation | pure: never touches the DOM; reports facts on the bus |
| `js/ui/` | DOM, canvases, dialogs | reads sim state, calls sim functions, listens to the bus |

`js/main.js` boots `js/ui/app.js`, which creates or loads a game and runs a `GameLoop` (`sim/game.js`) from `requestAnimationFrame`.

## State

One plain, serializable object holds the game. Keys starting with `$` are runtime-only (caches, per-minute scratch, an open comms channel) and are stripped from saves. `sim/save.js` saves and loads it. A save from any other schema (the current one is 7: v0.17 brought licenses, sub-factions and the powers' relations; 6 was v0.12's interiors, stations and orbits) is refused with a message to start a new game; there are no migrations. A save of the current schema is brought up to date in place (`upgradeState`) for anything that can be filled in.

## The minute

`minuteTick` (sim/game.js) advances one game-minute in a fixed order, the same at 1 min/s and at 1 day/s:

0. The gravity epoch is set to the minute (`setGravityEpoch`): everything flown this minute falls under the bodies where they are now (gravity.js).
1. Machinery limits (and the 0.1 G cap inside a station's gate, lifted while pulling up off a rock); the dock moves along its phases; the autopilot or a hand on the stick decides the command, with the terrain guard over it (terrain.js).
2. **Our ship flies** (`flightStep`; in a world's air, then dragged by it: worlds.js `airDrag`), or, held by a dock, flies the rest of the minute second by second in the berth's frame (`finishMinute`).
3. Clock +1; the world brings entities near us up to date (`world.tick`).
4. **NPC ships fly** on the commands they chose last minute, their pilots' terrain guard over them (`moveAgents`); one that came down on a rock is wrecked there.
5. Station-keeping (holding formation with a station, a ship, a convoy, a slot), or the dock puts us where it holds us (`dockHold`); either costs the thrust gravity doesn't give.
6. **Ordnance flies** and is checked against its targets over the minute just flown (`combatMove`): intercepts, hits, detonations; rounds that meet the ground are spent. Then, if we flew free, the ground (`terrainTick`: call-outs, contact). A drone out keeps up, or is left behind by this minute's thrust (`dronesTick`), and is called home for a burn coming. Then where we are among the worlds (`worldsTick`: planetary space, its layer, the airspace below; worlds.js).
7. Inertial events, then people: G-loads, physiology, crew AI, needs; careers (the dead remembered, a captain succeeded; once a day ages, ranks due, retirements, morale beyond the needs; pay day; those signing off when docked: careers.js).
8. Plant: fires (fire.js), life support, reactor. A reactor that goes supercritical destroys the ship.
9. Sensors (contacts that drop off the board go onto the plot) and comms; traffic control (zones, rules, the radio); the dossier's thermal counts.
10. **The wider world**: combat decisions (NPCs choose next minute's command, fire weapons, make demands; our PDCs strafe), law, politics (daily: relations, influence, feuds, proxies, zones) and the encounter director, our service (call-ups, reporting aboard, daily reputation), distress calls, contracts (military work among them), the market (every six hours: made and used, shipped, shocks: market.js), traffic, the background forces (hulls that came within reach wake; daily, the census and the yards), the intel inbox (digests are published, reports that have arrived at light speed are delivered), and last the captain's flags (one whose track the plot has let go goes too; one back on the board is called out).

In step 10 also: station defenses, a boarding's minute, our shuttles' runs (a phase begun, freight landed, a shuttle home or chasing us: shuttles.js) and the NPC shuttles where we can see them, crew back from leave, our berth's lease.

All ships move before any torpedo is resolved, and every decision made in step 10 is flown in the next minute. This keeps cause and effect strictly ordered.

**The one exception inside a minute** is docking (docking.js): from the final approach on, our ship's place relative to the berth is integrated in whole seconds (`dock.t`). The game loop may fly those seconds one at a time at a slower rate (hand-flying), and the minute tick flies whatever is left of the minute, so headless runs and the browser agree. Nothing else changes inside a minute.

## Motion models (world.js)

| Model | Used by | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| `orbit`: analytic, on rails around a body | stations, parked ships, nav marks, wrecks off a body | O(1) at any time |
| `transit`: analytic brachistochrone between two rails entities, or from a coasting fix | NPC traffic, background forces | O(1) |
| free: integrated under solar gravity and constant thrust | drifting hulks | substeps |
| `driven`: stepped every minute by a controller | NPC ships with a pilot (agents.js), torpedoes and slugs (ordnance.js) | bounded count |
| `flightPath`: legs on rails round a world (in a carrier, a powered spiral, an entry or a climb flown once as physics and looked up, on the ground, a free orbit), planetary.js; in its bay a shuttle is `$inBay`, part of its carrier, and the sensors and pictures pass it by | shuttles, ours and the NPCs' we can see | bounded count, O(log n) a look-up (a spiral's arc-length table, ARC_N steps, made once per leg) |

The world indexes entities by id, in a spatial hash, and keeps small sets: stations, distress beacons, driven entities, and the background forces (`world.forces`, a few hundred hulls). `world.driven` is the only set the per-minute combat code iterates. Traffic control and station defenses look only at `world.stations` (a busy port's hash cell holds thousands of ships). The forces' wake check walks `world.forces` once a minute.

## Level of detail and scale

The target is more than 200,000 entities. The rules:

- Entities near us (within `HORIZON_AU`, 0.105 AU, just past the longest sensor and radio reach) refresh every full minute. The far world keeps a **calendar** (`world.background` serves it): each entity is visited when its visitors next have business with it (`WORLD_DUE`, alongside `WORLD_VISITORS`: a leg's end, a call's next step, a force's arrival, a retirement), and its position is brought up to date only before it could come within the horizon: the gap closed at the speed between us, growing at its drive's G and ours (a G at least, or what we burn at), a day at most. So a frame does what is due in it, at any time warp, and a far ship's place is at most a day old, a near one's exact. Code that changes an entity's timing outside a visit calls `world.plan(e)` (`setDriven(e, false)` does); a slow round-robin pass, the whole world once a game day (at most 1,000 a frame), is the net under anything that didn't. Ports keep runtime indexes of their claims (holding.js `$hold`, the first slot that may be free; stations.js `$bh`, the berths taken; both counted afresh hourly), and the plot its filed plans by port (intel.js `plansTo`), so a crowd is asked about in O(1), not walked.
- Traffic never thinks. It becomes an **agent** (a driven ship with an AI) only when something near us needs it to act: a pirate picks it as prey, we open fire on it, a contract needs it.
- The background forces (police, navies, pirates: forces.js) are on rails too, and wake as agents within 0.1 AU of us. Agents from the population go back on rails when their business is done and we are far away (traffic woken by a fight, once it has nothing left to fear for seven hours, near us or not: a captain parked at a port for years would otherwise collect every calmed hull round it); walk-ons from the director and contracts leave the simulation.
- Conflict away from us has outcomes, not simulations: each hour a zone may cost one of the real ships on station there, or one in its lanes, which becomes a wreck.
- What the player knows is bounded too: the plot holds at most 6,000 reports, digests are capped at 400 ships, and the inbox holds a few dozen items in flight (intel.js).
- Ordnance exists only where it was launched. The count is small, and every torpedo has a lifetime.

`tests/game.test.mjs › scale` checks the per-minute budget at 200k ships (about 6 ms per game-minute with ~25,000 of them within sensor range of Ceres; since v0.17 the names, which take a lot of short-lived string work to make 200k of, lead V8's pretenuring heuristics to slow the later minutes by about a quarter, measured and left: it is a constant, not a growth), and `tests/intel.test.mjs › scale` the default system (3,000 traffic, the forces): about 0.06 ms per game-minute, and a 2.5 MB save. The 200k timing is sensitive to machine load (another memory-heavy process doubles it). In the browser at 200k, held at 1 day/s: 55–60 fps docked at Ceres and on the exterior display (the sim's frame about 11 ms: 23 lite minutes, a full one, the calendar's due and the net), about 42 on the map (its drawing at that rate on top); 60 at 1× everywhere. A year at 200k holds its frame flat (14–16 ms headless from day 15 to 365, no growth in the calendar, the ports' queues or the plot); a default game played at 1 day/s for 50 years, resupplied, holds at about 0.1 ms a game minute from its fifteenth year (the forces near a captain who never leaves wake to their caps), its save at 3.9 MB. A 200k save is about 150 MB, written in about half a second.

## Events

The sim emits facts (`torpedo-hit`, `crime`, `comm-request`, …). `ui/presenter.js` is the one place that decides how a fact is shown (log, toast, dialog). `ui/fx.js` keeps positioned combat effects for the canvases.

## UI modules (`js/ui/`)

The screen is the ship's flight console: a top rail, the main view (the ship's displays, under their tab bar and the flight band) between two docks of panels, the ship's AI and the radio along the bottom, and floating windows over it all. Nothing floats over a display but the card for what is picked on it: the nav status, traffic control, the route's progress, prompts and messages are lines of the layout (the flight band), and every display places its names through one pass that never prints one over another (`labels.js`). None of it touches the simulation.
